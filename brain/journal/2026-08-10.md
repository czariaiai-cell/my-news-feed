## [Gaza and diplomacy] Israel rejects US plan unless Hamas disarms first
Prime Minister [[Benjamin Netanyahu]] says [[Israel]] will not withdraw its forces from [[Gaza]] until [[Hamas]] is “genuinely” disarmed, rejecting the sequencing implied by the US-backed 15-point plan. Hamas says it is ready to implement the plan but links handing over heavy weapons to “all forms of aggression” ending and Israeli troop withdrawal. The US Board of Peace says the plan remains the only route forward and describes the process as phased, verifiable and reversible, but Netanyahu says negotiations with Washington are continuing and that some US ideas are unacceptable. This is a material development from the earlier plan: the core dispute is now explicit sequencing, not merely whether a framework exists. The UN estimates that about **1.9 million people**, or 90% of Gaza’s population, have been displaced; casualty figures cited by the BBC rely on Gaza’s health ministry and are not an independently audited battlefield count. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c5yw4lpe0yeo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).

## [Germany and defence] Drones spotted above base reportedly holding Patriot components
Two drones were seen above a Bundeswehr base in Mechernich, western [[Germany]], days after an explosive-laden device was found at Leipzig/Halle airport. Military police attended before the case was passed to local police. German media report that the base stores equipment and spare parts for US-made [[Patriot]] air-defence batteries, which are important to [[Ukraine]]’s interception of Russian ballistic missiles. Interior Minister Alexander Dobrindt called the pattern part of daily “hybrid warfare”, but the Bundeswehr has not identified who operated the drones and [[Russia]] denies involvement in similar incidents. The new sighting matters for [[Polska|Poland]] because it combines protection of military logistics with airspace monitoring and attribution: suspicion is not proof, and the Leipzig investigation is still open. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cwyeg1ljp2eo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).

## [Ecuador and organised crime] Former interior minister charged over candidate’s assassination
Prosecutors in [[Ecuador]] have charged former interior minister José Serrano and six others over the alleged indirect role in the 2023 assassination of presidential candidate Fernando Villavicencio. Five people linked to the Los Lobos gang were convicted in 2024; the new case reaches alleged organisers and political connections rather than the convicted hit team. Prosecutors say several defendants are fugitives in the United States and Venezuela, while gang leader Wilmer “Pipo” Chavarria is in Spain and faces an extradition request. A judge must still decide whether the prosecution has enough evidence for a full trial. The case shows how organised-crime violence can penetrate electoral institutions, but the charges are allegations and do not establish guilt. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cgjeznj6979o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).

## [Puerto Rico and climate resilience] Water rationing affects 180,000 properties
Authorities in [[Puerto Rico]] are rotating 48-hour water shut-offs for about **180,000 properties** after the Carraízo reservoir was depleted. The island experienced its driest July on record, and the US Drought Monitor classifies most of the territory as facing moderate to severe drought; officials say 68% of the island is currently under drought conditions. The water authority says approximately **65%** of produced water is lost or unbilled, much of it through leaks and ageing pipes, so the emergency reflects both weather stress and infrastructure weakness. Residents have queued at tanker trucks, and some communities reported uncertainty about when supply would return. For [[Polska|Poland]], the practical lesson is that drought planning must include leakage control, clear outage schedules and backup distribution, not only reservoir forecasts. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cqlxgk7r2vwo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).

## [Technology and work] AI companies promise shorter weeks while employees report extreme hours
Workers at AI-focused companies told the BBC that development “sprints” can reach **70–90 hours per week**, despite executives presenting AI as a route to a shorter working week. A former OpenAI employee said the company urged others to test a four-day week but did not run that trial internally; current and former employees at Meta described nights, weekends and being moved onto urgent AI teams. A UC Berkeley study cited by the BBC found that workers using AI completed tasks faster, broadened their workload and extended work into more hours of the day. These accounts are interviews and one study, not a universal measure of the whole sector, but they challenge the assumption that productivity gains automatically become free time. The family-relevant question is who controls the boundary between assistance and permanent availability. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cvgx4yd1gl2o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).

## [Children and social media] New Mexico judge orders Meta to pay $567m
A New Mexico judge ordered [[Meta]] to pay **$567m** for failing to warn about dangers its platforms posed to children, on top of an earlier **$375m** award in the same case. The judge labelled the company a “public nuisance” and ordered a fund: **$420m** is designated for treatment and behavioural-health programmes, with further money for prevention and training. The order also requires measures including preventing adult recommendations to under-18 accounts, restricting adult messaging, limiting certain notifications and imposing a 90-hour monthly usage cap for affected users. Meta denies the ruling’s characterisation and says it will appeal, so the final legal position may change. This is directly relevant to families because the court’s remedies target recommendation systems, contact safeguards and sleep/school-time notifications rather than only the size of the fine. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cd7lz3wr2rlo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).
