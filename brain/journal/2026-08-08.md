## [Ukraine and Russia] Russian strikes near Kyiv kill three, including a child
At least **three people, including a child, were killed and three wounded** in overnight ballistic-missile strikes near [[Kijów|Kyiv]], according to the head of the regional military administration. Falling debris damaged apartment buildings and cars, while fires affected a building and a fuel tank. This is a material new attack from yesterday’s interceptor-shortage assessment: the shortage is now accompanied by a fresh casualty report, not merely a repetition of the earlier warning. The figures are attributed to Ukrainian officials and emergency services and may change as rescue work continues. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cz7dy8gq99eo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).

## [Ukraine and Russia] Russian drones are targeting medics and ambulances in Kherson
A BBC investigation describes repeated drone attacks on emergency workers and vehicles in [[Chersoń|Kherson]]. One paramedic said an FPV drone hit her ambulance and another pursued her for about **10 minutes**; two further vehicles sent to evacuate the wounded woman were also hit. The [[WHO]] says it has registered **3,118 attacks on healthcare** in Ukraine since Russia’s full-scale invasion began in February 2022—about two per day by its calculation. These are documented attacks on healthcare infrastructure and personnel, but individual attribution and intent still require the normal evidentiary scrutiny. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c3r073eqvrjo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).

## [Middle East and defence] Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan sign a mutual-defence pact
[[Arabia Saudyjska|Saudi Arabia]], [[Turcja|Turkey]] and [[Pakistan]] signed the Mecca Joint Defence Agreement, stating that an armed attack on one will be regarded as an attack on all three. The joint statement says the pact is intended to strengthen collective deterrence and broaden defence cooperation, but it does **not specify** the forces, timelines or obligations that would follow an attack. A Turkish official described it as purely defensive, while an Iranian parliamentarian criticised the arrangement. Its significance is therefore political and deterrent for now, rather than proof of an integrated military command. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c30418m4mj4o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).

## [European migration] Spain and Italy impose reciprocal border controls over Ceuta crisis
Spain introduced checks on air and sea travel from Italy for one month, after Italy imposed similar restrictions following an influx of about **78,000 migrants** from Morocco into the Spanish exclave of [[Ceuta]]. Italy says its controls for non-EU nationals will last at least until **15 August** and cites security and terrorist-risk concerns; Madrid says the measures rest on arguments without a factual basis. This is a significant new development from yesterday’s report on migrants remaining in Ceuta: the issue has escalated into a dispute over the [[Schengen]] border-free regime between two EU states. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c5yv5l6yr5ko?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).

## [United States and energy] RWE abandons US offshore wind projects after $1.2bn payout
German energy company [[RWE]] said it will abandon offshore-wind leases off California, Louisiana and in the New York Bight after agreeing to a **$1.2bn** payout from the US Department of the Interior. It plans to reinvest **$900m** in a Louisiana LNG export-terminal project, while saying its broader US investment plan is about **€17bn over six years**. The deal is a concrete shift of capital from offshore wind to gas, not evidence that all US renewable projects are being cancelled. It also shows how regulatory decisions can alter European companies’ energy exposure and American infrastructure priorities. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c1e1vg0gjl5o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).

## [Critical infrastructure] A 90-second power outage disrupts rail services across north-west England
A local power cut affecting Network Rail’s Manchester operating centre was resolved within about **90 seconds**, but signalling and communications systems did not recover cleanly. Trains had to stop, systems were rebooted and damaged signalling components had to be replaced, leaving crews and rolling stock out of position across the North West. Network Rail disputes the description of a full 90-second interruption but acknowledges that some systems behaved unexpectedly. The incident is a useful resilience case for [[Polska|Poland]]: backup power is not enough if control, communications and recovery procedures are not independently tested. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c3ekd5784k5o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).

## [United States economy] US payrolls fall by 23,000 as July data are revised down
Official US figures showed a surprise loss of **23,000 jobs** in July, against forecasts for about **80,000 new jobs**. May and June payroll growth was revised down by a combined **103,000**; unemployment edged from 4.2% to **4.1%**, while annual wage growth slowed to **3.2%**. The figures reduced market expectations of a near-term Federal Reserve rate increase, although one month—especially July—does not establish a recession. For Europe, weaker US demand and changing interest-rate expectations matter more than the single headline number. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c20j5rle717o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).

## [Technology and Saudi investment] Saudi-led group completes $55bn purchase of Electronic Arts
A group including Saudi Arabia’s [[Public Investment Fund]], Affinity Partners and Jared Kushner’s investment firm completed the **$55bn** purchase of [[Electronic Arts]], taking the game publisher private. The buyers reportedly used **$36bn** of their own capital and **$20bn** in borrowing, leaving EA with substantial debt. The deal covers franchises including EA FC, The Sims and Mass Effect and is described as the largest leveraged buyout in history, though its final ranking and longer-term consequences should be treated cautiously. The immediate strategic issue is whether debt servicing drives layoffs, monetisation changes or concentration on established franchises. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cjejyl34345o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).
