## Key developments

## [Israel and West Bank] Archaeological site plan would expropriate about 450 acres
Israeli authorities are taking possession of roughly **450 acres** around the ancient site of Sebastia in the occupied [[West Bank]], saying the purpose is to protect and develop a national park. Palestinian landowners and a local restaurant owner fear loss of land, access and livelihoods; [[UNESCO]] added Sebastia to its World Heritage List and the List of World Heritage in Danger on **25 July**. UNESCO identifies the planned expropriation and proposed “Samaria” national park as a threat that could divide the site. The competing legal and historical claims are politically charged, so the reported plans should not be treated as a final ruling on sovereignty. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c20d8qre98do?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).

## [France and climate resilience] Wildfire arrests reach 420 after record-damage summer
French authorities say **420 people**, including **166 minors**, have been arrested in connection with this summer’s wildfires; more than **300,000 acres** have burned, according to the BBC report. The government says nine out of ten French wildfires are started by people, but lawyers and journalists question whether the rapid arrests distinguish deliberate arson from accidents and whether the response was adequately prepared. One alleged arsonist faces up to **15 years in prison** and a **€150,000** fine if convicted. Arrest totals are official figures, not proof that every suspect caused a fire. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cvgxjn92x9jo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).

## [United States justice system] Senate confirms Todd Blanche as attorney general by 50–49
The US Senate confirmed [[Donald Trump]]’s former personal lawyer Todd Blanche as attorney general by **50–49**, with Republican senators Susan Collins and Lisa Murkowski joining Democrats in opposition. Blanche had served as acting attorney general since April. The confirmation puts a former presidential defence lawyer in charge of the [[United States]]’ top law-enforcement department; the central concern raised by opponents was politicisation and whether he would check the administration’s “worst impulses”. His proposed **$1.8bn** anti-“weaponisation” compensation fund was reportedly walked back after Republican objections, but the report says it remains unclear whether that commitment is legally binding. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cewr898jy8go?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).

## [United States and Colombia] Washington offers a $1bn security package to Colombia’s new president
The [[United States]] pledged **$1bn** to [[Kolumbia|Colombia]] shortly after right-wing president Abelardo de la Espriella took office. The State Department describes the package as supporting shared goals of fighting organised crime and illegal immigration. De la Espriella promised an “all-out war” on drug cartels and said the state would act “by reason or by force”; his approach contrasts with predecessor Gustavo Petro’s “total peace” strategy of negotiations and incentives for farmers. The amount and political alignment are clear, but the report does not yet establish how the money will be allocated or whether the tougher strategy will reduce violence. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cy9wy3y0e5wo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).

## [East Asia and infrastructure] Typhoon Dolphin cuts power to 44,000 buildings in Japan
Typhoon Dolphin hit [[Japonia|Japan]]’s Okinawa with maximum winds of **162 km/h**, cutting electricity to at least **44,000 buildings** and injuring at least **five people**. Naha airport cancelled flights, while ports, ferries, schools and an airport in China’s Zhejiang province were closed ahead of the storm’s expected landfall on the eastern Chinese coast. The injuries reported by Okinawa’s government were non-life-threatening. For [[Polska|Poland]], the relevant lesson is operational resilience: transport, electricity and port shutdowns can become cross-border supply-chain disruptions even when casualties remain limited. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cvglp2zlvrlo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).

## [Canada and climate resilience] British Columbia wildfire doubles to 95 sq km and forces evacuations
The Bald Range fire in British Columbia grew to more than **95 sq km** and remained “out of control” on Saturday, according to the BC Wildfire Service. Evacuation orders affected Summerland, a town of about **12,000** people, as well as nearby districts; hundreds of residents from care facilities were relocated and power was cut. The incident is a new event, not a repeat of the French wildfire report: it shows how quickly a fire can move from a remote zone into a direct housing, health-care and electricity emergency. The number of destroyed structures was not yet known in the BBC report. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cx25dkwk3e3o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).

## [Technology and sport] FIFA abandons plan to sell minority stake in World Cup
[[FIFA]] reversed a plan to sell a minority stake in the World Cup after opposition, boycott threats and calls for president Gianni Infantino to resign. The proposed investment was backed by a technology-investor group linked to Thrive Capital and was based on the argument that live sport, culture and identity are difficult for [[AI]] to replicate. The episode matters beyond football: it is a concrete example of financial investors trying to monetise scarce cultural assets while governance and fan legitimacy impose limits. The report does not indicate that the proposal will return in its original form. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cd7l4e3v238o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).
