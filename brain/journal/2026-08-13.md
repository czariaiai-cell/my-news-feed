## [Ukraine and Russia] Black Sea strike damages Russian grain terminals and naval infrastructure
A “massive” Ukrainian drone-and-missile attack hit the port of [[Novorossiysk]], one of [[Russia]]’s principal Black Sea ports, overnight on 12–13 August. Local officials reported **three deaths, including an eight-year-old child, and 24 injuries**. Two major grain-export terminals were damaged, according to reporting by the BBC and statements from grain companies to Russian and international news agencies. President [[Volodymyr Zelenskyy]] said Ukraine used rockets, aircraft and naval drones in a “unique operation”; Ukrainian officials said **four Russian warships**, including two frigates, were hit, but Russia had not publicly confirmed that claim in the BBC report. Moscow’s agriculture ministry said it was redirecting cargo toward Baltic and Caspian ports and land routes, citing logistical constraints. The incident matters beyond the military target: disruption at Novorossiysk can affect Russian wheat exports, Black Sea shipping risk and food-market expectations. The report also says Russia’s August exports could be more than half below the five-year average, although that estimate comes from a Moscow-based analytics company and is not an independently verified final total. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c5y49xp2wrno?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).

## [Russia and Japan] Putin visit to disputed Kuril island triggers formal protest
[[Japan]] said it “strongly protests” [[Vladimir Putin]]’s visit to a disputed [[Kuril Islands|Kuril island]]. Russian state media reported that Putin visited Iturup, called Etorofu in Japan, on 13 August. Japanese Foreign Minister Toshimitsu Motegi said the islands are “inherently Japanese territory” historically and under international law. The territorial dispute has lasted for decades and was a principal reason that Moscow and Tokyo never signed a post-World War Two peace treaty. The immediate consequence is diplomatic rather than military, but the visit is a deliberate signal of Russian control over territory claimed by Japan and comes against a wider deterioration in Russia–Japan relations. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cqx7ple0nxxo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).

## [Ukraine and Russian politics] Nobel laureate warns that Kremlin cannot turn the war into a victory
[[Dmitry Muratov]], the 2021 Nobel Peace Prize co-winner and former editor of the independent Russian newspaper Novaya Gazeta, told the BBC that [[Vladimir Putin]] can no longer credibly claim victory in [[Ukraine]]. Muratov said the paper’s printing presses had been nationalised, its registration revoked and its website blocked inside Russia. He estimated that at least **1,500 people** are now imprisoned for opposing state policy, roughly three times the number he associated with the late Soviet KGB period, although this is his estimate rather than an official prison census. He also said discussions of using nuclear weapons had appeared more than **500 times** in Russian public debate since the start of 2026, while stressing that nobody can know what Putin personally intends. The interview is not evidence of a change in Kremlin policy; its value is as a first-hand account of censorship and as a warning about escalation rhetoric. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c74grmy2v7ko?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).

## [Democratic Republic of Congo and public health] WHO warns Ebola outbreak could exceed the historic death toll
The World Health Organization’s director-general, Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus, said the 2026 [[Ebola]] outbreak in the [[Democratic Republic of Congo]] is on course, at its current pace, to exceed the **at least 11,000 deaths** recorded in the 2014–2016 West African epidemic. The outbreak was officially declared on **15 May** and has so far involved at least **4,300 cases and more than 2,000 deaths**. WHO officials believe transmission began at least three months earlier and say only about **30% of cases** are being reached by health workers because of regional insecurity. This is the rare Bundibugyo species; unlike the Zaire species, it has no approved vaccine or recognised therapeutic drug. The UK regulator has authorised first-in-human trials of an Oxford-developed vaccine, and the WHO is sponsoring a trial of two existing antiviral therapies. This is a serious outbreak warning, not a prediction that the final death toll will necessarily exceed 2014–2016: WHO’s statement is explicitly conditional on the current pace continuing. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cgk4pex4mn8o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).

## [United States and cost of living] Inflation slows slightly, but prices continue to rise
US consumer prices rose **3.4%** in the year to July, down marginally from **3.5%** in June, according to new Bureau of Labor Statistics figures reported by the BBC. Month-on-month inflation was **0.1%**, with housing costs the main upward pressure. Gasoline prices fell **2.9%** from June to July but were still **24.6% higher year-on-year**. Food prices rose only slightly and energy prices fell during the month, while prices excluding food and energy increased **0.2%**; medical care and airline tickets rose, while car insurance fell. The practical distinction for households is important: disinflation means that prices are increasing more slowly, not that the previous price increases have been reversed. The figures leave the Federal Reserve room to wait rather than react to a renewed acceleration, but they do not by themselves determine the next interest-rate decision. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c0qv2nn1gpeo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).

## [Brazil and child safety online] Discord ordered to suspend livestreaming
Brazil’s data-protection authority, ANPD, ordered [[Discord]] to suspend its livestreaming function after investigating the death of a **13-year-old girl** who was allegedly pressured by people in a Discord server to take her own life. The investigation began on **7 August**. Discord said it had shut down the private server, was cooperating with law enforcement and found evidence that activity had also been coordinated on other platforms. The company has three days to comply and can appeal within ten business days; the authority said penalties could reach **50 million reais (about $9.67m) per violation**. Five teenagers have reportedly been arrested, but the BBC article does not present a final judicial finding establishing all responsibility. The order will remain until Discord demonstrates adequate protections for minors, potentially including age verification. For families, the case shows why private groups, livestreaming and moderation access are relevant safety issues even when a platform has formal reporting rules. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cgewpqxyrddo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).

## [Japan and productivity] Slow workplace AI adoption collides with labour shortages
Only **8.4%** of Japanese workers reportedly use [[AI]] as part of their job, compared with separate figures of **50% in the United States** and **32% in the United Kingdom**, according to statistics cited by the BBC from OECD and other sources. Japan faces an ageing population, labour shortages and weak productivity, which would normally create strong incentives for automation. Businesses and researchers interviewed by the BBC attribute the slow uptake to conservative decision-making, low tolerance for errors, reputational risk and incomplete workplace digitisation; some hospitals still rely heavily on paper patient records. The government’s AI Promotion Act is intended to encourage adoption through light-touch regulation. The figures are not perfectly comparable because they come from different datasets and definitions of “using AI”, but the direction is clear: Japan’s cautious approach may reduce immediate error risk while also delaying productivity gains needed to address demographic pressure. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cwymw4434v7o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).
