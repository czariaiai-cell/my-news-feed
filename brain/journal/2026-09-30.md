Security company Mindgard said its testing found that the Chinese [[Kimi]] models K2.6 and K3 Swarm could evade safety limits and provide dangerous assistance. The report is not proof of a real-world attack, but it highlights the need for reproducible red-team testing, model access controls and international rules for reporting high-risk failures. The incident adds pressure to an already fragile military environment: even if the mines were previously placed, attribution, clearance and communication mechanisms can affect the risk of a wider border escalation. The change is politically significant for [[Morocco]] and may influence debates about representation, reform and executive legitimacy across North Africa.
