## [Colombia and disaster response] Death toll rises as rescuers search for survivors
The death toll from Monday’s magnitude **7.4** earthquake in [[Kolumbia|Colombia]] rose to at least **181**, according to the official figure reported by the BBC; an aggregation of local officials’ figures was potentially above **240**. President Abelardo de la Espriella said almost **2,600 people were injured**, nearly **200 remained missing**, more than **1,100 homes were destroyed** and over **8,000 damaged**. In Cali, the mayor reported **239 people still trapped**, **949 injured** and 45 buildings completely or partially collapsed; a hospital’s upper floors, including paediatric areas, were among the damaged structures. Aftershocks, curfews and blocked or damaged access routes complicate rescue and shelter. This is a material update to yesterday’s earthquake report: the casualty count, missing-person estimate, building damage and confirmed rescue constraints have all increased. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c20dqd9qwq4o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).

## [United States and Iran] US missiles disable ship accused of breaching blockade
[[United States|US Central Command]] says an MH-60 helicopter fired two Hellfire missiles into the engine room or steering systems of the Panama-flagged **M/V Vela Nova** after its civilian crew ignored repeated warnings while attempting to reach an Iranian port through the Gulf of Oman. Centcom said the ship was no longer transiting to [[Iran]] and reported that, as of 11 August, US forces had redirected **55 commercial vessels**, disabled **three** and boarded **two**. The command did not report casualties, and Iran had not commented when the BBC article was published. The incident is strategically important because it turns the blockade from a maritime warning regime into a direct use-of-force problem for commercial shipping. Centcom’s account is the available description; independent confirmation of the engagement and the vessel’s condition is limited. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cvg8lyyyjedo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).

## [United States and presidential security] Trump confirms a secret plane swap after a possible missile threat
President [[Donald Trump]] confirmed that the [[Secret Service]] and military moved him from the publicly visible Air Force One to another aircraft after the [[NATO]] summit in Turkey in July. CBS sources told the BBC that US intelligence had detected a credible Iranian threat to fire a missile at the aircraft, while the New York Times reported information about a person near the summit allegedly seen with a shoulder-fired missile. Trump boarded the decoy plane in view of cameras and was then moved in a catering truck; journalists and some White House staff on the decoy aircraft reportedly did not know he had left. The account is politically and operationally significant, but “possible” or “credible” threat descriptions are not the same as proof that an attack was attempted. The story is a new disclosure about the earlier incident, not a repeat of yesterday’s industrial-war and drone coverage. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c980r4wpl9lo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).

## [Lebanon and rule of law] Parliament votes to abolish the death penalty
[[Lebanon]]’s 128-seat parliament voted by a majority to abolish capital punishment; the bill now goes to the cabinet and then to President Joseph Aoun for signature. Hezbollah’s parliamentary bloc voted against it. Lebanon has not carried out an execution since **January 2004**, but courts have continued to issue death sentences and rights groups estimate that more than **78 people** remain on death row. The proposed replacement is life imprisonment with hard labour, although the legislation does not yet specify how that sentence would operate in a country whose financial crisis has left prisons overcrowded and under-resourced. The vote is therefore a major legal step, not yet a completed abolition. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c8enj8p1xwgo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).

## [Hungary and Europe] Former European human-rights judge elected president
Hungary’s parliament elected [[András Baka]], 73, as president by **140 votes to 6**. Baka previously served as Hungary’s judge at the [[European Court of Human Rights]] and as head of its Supreme Court before Viktor Orbán’s government removed him in 2011. The presidency is largely ceremonial, but Baka is expected to oversee the drafting of a new constitution as Prime Minister Péter Magyar’s government dismantles parts of the Orbán-era system. The government has cancelled the states of emergency used to rule by decree, abolished the Sovereignty Protection Office and created an asset-recovery body with powers to investigate alleged theft of state funds. All 51 Fidesz MPs walked out before the vote, and Fidesz calls the new government’s changes a “tyranny”. The institutional direction is significant for the EU, but the new president’s ability to affect policy remains constrained by the office’s constitutional powers. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/clyed7rem5no?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).

## [France and consumer protection] Unsolicited telemarketing calls are banned
[[France]] has prohibited unsolicited business telemarketing calls from 11 August, except where a customer has an existing contract with the company or has given prior consent. A 2025 parliamentary report found that **97%** of people were annoyed by such calls; **72%** said they were contacted on a mobile at least weekly and **38%** daily. Consumer groups call the change a “small revolution”, while business representatives warn about administrative burdens and Moroccan officials fear losses in a call-centre sector serving the French market. The law requires companies to obtain and retain proof of written consent. For families, the practical point is that a cold call is no longer automatically legitimate merely because it comes from a business; the exact exceptions and enforcement rules still matter. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cy5d11g72ppo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).

## [Science and practical safety] Partial solar eclipse reaches Europe
A partial [[solar eclipse]] will be visible across Europe on 12 August, with up to **96%** of the Sun covered in parts of the UK; totality is expected in parts of Spain, Greenland, Iceland, Portugal and Russia. The BBC reports that specialist eclipse glasses were nearly sold out, but ordinary sunglasses do not protect eyes from concentrated sunlight. Anyone observing the event should use certified solar viewers and follow the instructions for the specific product; cameras, binoculars and telescopes require appropriate solar filters. This is a practical family-safety item rather than a geopolitical development, included because the source provides a time-sensitive, evidence-based warning relevant to children and adults. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cx2jr1ew2v1o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).
