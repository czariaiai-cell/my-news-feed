## [Colombia and disaster response] Earthquake kills at least 132 and triggers state of emergency
A magnitude **7.4** earthquake struck [[Kolumbia|Colombia]] at 07:34 local time, at a depth of **103 km**, according to the country’s geological service. At least **132 people were killed** and more than **480 injured**; the BBC reports that the death toll may rise because people are still feared trapped. Pereira’s metropolitan area recorded at least 40 deaths, 65 collapsed buildings and 15 buildings where people may remain trapped. Cali reported 32 collapsed buildings. The epicentre was near San José del Palmar in Chocó province, where damaged roads complicated access, while a curfew was imposed in parts of Pereira. The immediate operational issue is not only rescue capacity but also access, building assessment and communication during aftershocks. This is a new disaster, not a repeat of yesterday’s Colombia security-package item. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c20e360lx0vo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).

## [Russia and Ukraine] Ukrainian drone strike kills at least 13 in Tatarstan
At least **13 people**, including one child, were killed and **75 injured** when Ukrainian drones struck Nizhnekamsk in Russia’s Tatarstan republic, according to local officials. The city lies more than **1,100 km** from Ukraine; Ukraine’s Armed Forces said the Tanevo oil refinery was hit. The attack lasted several hours overnight and into the morning, and the BBC has not independently verified the Russian casualty account or all footage. Russian officials said many victims were in a hostel and described them as civilians; Kyiv argues that the targeted facilities contribute to Russia’s war effort and logistics. The strike is significant because it shows the expanding geographic reach of Ukrainian long-range attacks and the civilian-risk problem around industrial sites. Russian officials said those responsible would face punishment, but attribution and legal responsibility remain contested. This is a distinct development from the earlier Kyiv and front-line stories omitted from today’s cycle. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cvgjvgv926po?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).

## [United States and public health] Trump order recommends fewer childhood vaccines and separated MMR doses
US President [[Donald Trump]] signed an executive order recommending a reduction in the number of routine childhood vaccines from **18 to 11** and splitting the measles, mumps and rubella (MMR) doses. The order is guidance rather than a nationwide mandate because school-entry requirements are set by individual states. The BBC reports that multiple studies have found no link between MMR vaccination and autism, contradicting the president’s repeated claim. The recommended core list still includes measles, mumps, rubella, diphtheria, tetanus, pertussis, polio, Hib, pneumococcal disease, HPV and chickenpox. The practical family issue is that changes in federal guidance can create confusion even where state requirements remain unchanged; parents should check their state schedule and discuss individual contraindications with a qualified clinician. This is a new policy development, not a repeat of the previous health coverage. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/ce3q5vl581wo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).

## [AI infrastructure and finance] Nvidia assembles $500bn capital push for compute
[[Nvidia]] says it has teamed up with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR to raise **$500bn** for artificial-intelligence infrastructure. The investors are treating “compute”—chips, data centres and related infrastructure—as an asset class for the first time, according to the company. The funding is intended for Nvidia’s projects and partner projects, including facilities to house and cool large quantities of GPUs and factories that manufacture AI chips. The companies named as major users include Google, Meta, Amazon, Microsoft, SpaceX, Tesla, OpenAI and Anthropic. The concrete risk is execution: large financial commitments do not automatically produce enough electricity, cooling, grid connections or chips on schedule. For [[Polska|Poland]] and Europe, the development strengthens the case for asking where AI infrastructure will obtain power and how its costs affect grids and industrial policy. This is a new financing announcement and does not repeat yesterday’s AI-work-hours item. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c78gr0jv0mdo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).

## [Climate and oceans] July sea temperatures set a record as El Niño develops
Average sea-surface temperature outside the polar regions reached **20.96°C in July**, exceeding the previous July record of 20.89°C set in 2023, according to the [[Copernicus]] Climate Change Service. July was jointly the second-warmest globally, at **1.47°C above** the estimated pre-industrial average. Marine heatwaves were especially strong along Europe’s Atlantic coast and in the western Mediterranean, while El Niño conditions were developing in the tropical Pacific and are forecast to strengthen. Warmer seawater expands, accounting for roughly a third of observed sea-level rise, and marine heatwaves pressure ecosystems and fisheries. This matters for Europe through coastal risk, water and food systems, not only through a single weather event. It is a new measurement and ocean-scale development, distinct from yesterday’s Puerto Rico water-rationing report and the earlier wildfire stories. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cpvw8vmmgrwo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).
